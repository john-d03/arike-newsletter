01 · Cover story

Empowered to heal

The best care is a partnership, not a service delivered.

The Arike team recently had the privilege of caring for a 60 year old gentleman who had spent his earlier years working in stone masonry. An accident at work involving a stray stone had left him with a severe leg wound. Through medical care and careful self-management, he kept the injury well under control for a long time.

Then his wife fell ill. He gave all his time and energy to looking after her, and amidst the demands of caring for his family his own wound went unattended and became severely infected. That is when they reached out to Arike.

Our team began visiting him regularly to clean and dress the wound. As the visits became routine, he made a deeply thoughtful suggestion. He had noticed the daily workload of our nurses and the number of home-bound patients waiting for them.

“I know you have so many people waiting for you, people who might need you more than I do. If you teach me how to do this, I can manage the dressing myself, and you can visit every other day instead of daily.”

Our patient, to the nursing team

Valuing his determination, the team guided him and his wife through proper wound care. Today an Arike nurse visits to do the dressing on alternate days, and he and his family confidently manage it themselves on the days in between. The shared responsibility changed the couple's outlook completely, replacing their early anxiety with a real sense of capability.

This story shows why patient and family empowerment matters so much at Arike. By taking an active part in his own healing alongside his family, he reclaimed his independence, and he made sure our nursing team could reach other patients who needed them more. It reminds us that the best healthcare is a true partnership, built on empathy, education and the shared strength of a family.

 

Intern diaries

ഇന്റേൺമാരുടെ കുറിപ്പുകൾ

Sr. Nimmy · Rajagiri College

One of the most remarkable parts of Arike is the home-care service. I was deeply touched by the dedication of the nurses who visit patients at home and care for them in a familiar place. I saw nurses who went far beyond treating this as a job. They cared for each patient with the same affection they would show a member of their own family, taking the time to understand each person's circumstances, planning services around their needs, and working together to deliver them.

My fieldwork here became more than an academic requirement. It helped me understand compassion, teamwork, dignity, commitment and human-centred care, and it has left me with lessons I hope to carry into both my practice and my life.

Aneeta, Ananthu and Shamna · Don Bosco College, Mukkam

The one month internship was a valuable and meaningful learning experience. It gave us practical exposure to palliative care through home visits with the nurses and the wider team. We also ran a first-aid awareness class for NSS students at GVHSS North Edappally, and an awareness programme on basic health monitoring devices for members of a residents association.

Taking part in patient and caregiver interactions, day-care activities, documentation and telephone work strengthened our communication, observation, teamwork and empathy, and gave us a better understanding of the physical, emotional and social needs of patients and their families.

03 · Care info · for families at home

Sharing the caring within the family

പരിചരണ കുറിപ്പ്

Caring for a family member is demanding, and sharing the work makes it easier for everyone. When a family works together the patient gets steady support, and the load does not fall on one person alone.

01Talk openly as a family about the patient's care and the support that is needed.
02Share the daily jobs: going with the patient, preparing meals, helping with housework.
03Divide the work by what each person can realistically give in time and ability.
04Keep everyone informed when something changes in the patient's care or routine.

Small contributions count. Even a little help from each person lightens the load on the main caregiver and makes a real difference at home.
